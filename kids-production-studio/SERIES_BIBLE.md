# The Magic Letters Adventure — Series Bible v1

**Producer:** Daoline Books  
**Audience:** English-speaking absolute beginners learning Arabic as a foreign language, ages 4–7.  
**Language:** English for story comprehension; accurate Modern Standard Arabic for short teaching moments.  
**Season:** 28 letter adventures; a pilot short and optional finale.

## Approved recurring cast
- **Asad:** warm, curious golden lion cub. His name is also the Arabic target word in episode 1.
- **Jamal:** friendly camel and helpful problem-solver.
- **Zeina:** observant giraffe, introduced only when production references are approved.
- **Dodo:** cheerful original duck character, introduced only when production references are approved.

No human characters in the pilot. Characters speak directly to the viewer. Keep cast per shot small to reduce generation drift.

## Educational contract
- Start from zero Arabic knowledge; explain meaning in natural English.
- For letter 1 say **"This is Alif"**; show `ا` for the letter name as on the companion page and `أَ` when demonstrating the initial vowel in `أَسَد`. Do not imply these spellings are identical.
- Never render the target word as `A-sad`; use **Asad** as the romanized label, with Arabic `أَسَد` in a verified overlay.
- Name the letter, demonstrate its sound, teach one word, pause for repetition, recognize its written form, and invite tracing.
- Do not ask children to buy. Parent-directed book links belong in descriptions or parent-facing pages.
- Match all letter examples to the existing workbook and companion audio before approving a script.

## Visual production
- Begin with the existing 9:16 Asad-and-Jamal Grok clip and test a 10–15-second continuation.
- Build one approved character reference sheet before generating a longer episode.
- Generate video without spoken dialogue or AI-rendered Arabic text; add Arabic overlays and consistent voices in editing.
- Do not assume a landscape crop can produce a good portrait cut; produce formats separately.
- Use creator-owned music and effects.
- Maintain one golden-star motif and a friendly, cinematic savanna setting.

## Quality gates
1. Story clear without Arabic.
2. One memorable Arabic learning outcome.
3. Arabic verified against the book's page and audio.
4. No character-design drift or unexpected new characters.
5. Final audio understandable and pauses preserved.
6. Export and publication approved by the producer.
